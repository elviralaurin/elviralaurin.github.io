The Feynman Technique, named after physicist Richard Feynman, refers to a study technique in which you try to explain the thing you're trying to learn in simplified language. The idea is that if you understand a concept deeply enough, you should be able to explain it to someone with no knowledge of the topic.

On top of being a way of checking your knowledge, it can also be a good tool for learning new topics.

[Rubber duck debugging](https://rubberduckdebugging.com/) is a similar idea to the Feynman Technique, applied to software development. By explaining what you think you're doing or what you think you know (traditionally to a rubber duck), it's often easier to identify gaps in your understanding, allowing you to see what your next steps should be.

The Duck Notes are my attempt to teach myself new things by writing them down as if I'm trying to teach them to someone else.

By reading them, you take on the role of the imagined rubber duck that I pictured when I wrote them. Weirdly intimate, if you think about it.
